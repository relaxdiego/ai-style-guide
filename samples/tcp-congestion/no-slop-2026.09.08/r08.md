TCP congestion control is a control loop that guesses the network's spare capacity, because no router tells the sender what it is. The sender keeps a congestion window (cwnd), sends at most `min(cwnd, receiver_window)` bytes unacknowledged, and adjusts cwnd based on feedback: ACK arrival, duplicate ACKs, timeouts, or ECN marks.

## The classic loop (Reno, RFC 5681)

- **Slow start.** cwnd starts at ~10 MSS (Linux) and grows by 1 MSS per ACK, so it roughly doubles every RTT. Exponential probing, ends when cwnd crosses `ssthresh` or a loss occurs.
- **Congestion avoidance.** cwnd grows by about 1 MSS per RTT (additive increase). This is the steady-state probe for more bandwidth.
- **Fast retransmit / fast recovery.** Three duplicate ACKs mean a packet was lost but the pipe still flows. Retransmit immediately, set `ssthresh = cwnd/2`, set `cwnd = ssthresh` (multiplicative decrease), stay in congestion avoidance.
- **Timeout.** No ACKs at all. Assume severe congestion: `ssthresh = cwnd/2`, `cwnd = 1 MSS`, restart slow start.

Additive-increase/multiplicative-decrease is what makes competing flows converge to a roughly fair split of a bottleneck: the additive part gives everyone the same absolute gain, the multiplicative part takes proportionally more from whoever has more.

## Why the classic loop got replaced

Reno's throughput scales as roughly `MSS / (RTT * sqrt(p))` for loss rate `p`. On a 10 Gb/s link with 100 ms RTT, that needs a loss rate below about 10⁻¹⁰ to stay saturated, and recovery after a single loss takes over an hour. It also penalizes long-RTT flows, since increase is per-RTT.

| Algorithm | Signal | Behavior |
|---|---|---|
| CUBIC (Linux/Android default) | Loss | cwnd is a cubic function of time since the last loss, not of RTT. Fast recovery toward the previous window, then aggressive probing above it. RTT-fair. |
| BBR (Google, QUIC/YouTube) | Bandwidth + RTT estimates | Measures delivery rate and minimum RTT, sets a pacing rate at the estimated bottleneck. Ignores loss as a congestion signal, so it works over lossy links. |
| DCTCP | ECN mark fraction | Datacenter only. Reduces cwnd proportionally to how many packets got marked, giving small queues and low latency. |
| Vegas / Westwood | Delay / rate | Back off on RTT inflation before loss. Lose out badly when sharing a link with loss-based flows. |

## Supporting machinery

- **SACK** tells the sender exactly which segments arrived, so it can recover from multiple losses in one round trip instead of one per RTT.
- **PRR (RFC 6937)** spreads the window reduction over the recovery period instead of dropping cwnd in one step, avoiding a stall followed by a burst.
- **Pacing** spaces packets across the RTT rather than dumping the whole window at once. Required for BBR, and generally kind to shallow router buffers.
- **ECN** lets a router mark a packet instead of dropping it, giving congestion feedback without a retransmit.

The practical tension today is bufferbloat: loss-based algorithms only back off once a router buffer is full, so on an oversized buffer they drive latency into the hundreds of milliseconds while still reporting good throughput. That is what AQM (fq_codel, CAKE) and delay-aware algorithms like BBR exist to fix.
