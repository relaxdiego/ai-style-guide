TCP congestion control is a feedback loop that guesses how much data the network path can hold, because no one tells the sender. The sender keeps a **congestion window** (cwnd) of unacknowledged bytes it's willing to have in flight, raises it while ACKs keep arriving, and cuts it when it sees a congestion signal (loss, an ECN mark, or rising delay). Actual in-flight data is `min(cwnd, receiver's advertised window)`, so flow control and congestion control are separate limits.

## The classic state machine (Reno/NewReno)

- **Slow start.** cwnd begins at about 10 MSS on Linux (IW10) and increases by 1 MSS per ACK, so it doubles every RTT. Exponential, despite the name. Runs until cwnd crosses `ssthresh` or a loss happens.
- **Congestion avoidance.** cwnd grows by roughly 1 MSS per RTT (additive increase). This is the probing phase: creep upward until the path complains.
- **Fast retransmit / fast recovery.** Three duplicate ACKs mean a segment was lost but packets are still flowing. Retransmit immediately, set `ssthresh = cwnd/2`, set cwnd to that, and continue in congestion avoidance.
- **Timeout.** RTO fires when nothing is coming back at all. Treated as severe: cwnd drops to 1 MSS and slow start restarts. Costly, which is why SACK, RACK-TLP, and tail loss probe exist to convert timeouts into fast retransmits.

The increase/decrease rule is AIMD (additive increase, multiplicative decrease). AIMD is what makes competing flows converge toward equal shares: additive increase gives everyone the same absolute gain, multiplicative decrease takes proportionally more from the larger flow.

## Why Reno wasn't enough

Reno's throughput follows roughly `MSS / (RTT * sqrt(p))` for loss rate `p`. Two consequences:

- On a 10 Gbps, 100 ms path you need a loss rate near 10⁻¹⁰ to fill the pipe, and recovery after one loss takes thousands of RTTs.
- Flows with short RTTs beat flows with long RTTs on the same bottleneck, because increase is per-RTT.

## What's actually deployed

| Algorithm | Signal | Behavior |
|---|---|---|
| CUBIC (Linux default) | Loss | cwnd is a cubic function of time since the last reduction, so growth is fast when far from the previous peak and cautious near it. Multiplier is 0.7, not 0.5. RTT-independent, so it's fairer across mixed RTTs. |
| BBR (Google, YouTube) | Measured bottleneck bandwidth and min RTT | Estimates delivery rate and propagation delay, sets the sending rate directly, and paces packets. Ignores loss as a primary signal, so it doesn't collapse on lossy wireless links and doesn't fill deep buffers. |
| DCTCP | ECN marks | Datacenters only. Reduces cwnd proportionally to the fraction of marked packets rather than halving, keeping queues short. |
| Vegas | Rising RTT | Backs off before loss. Loses badly when sharing a bottleneck with loss-based flows, which is why it stayed academic. |

## The part people trip over

Loss-based control needs a full buffer to detect congestion. When routers have oversized buffers, CUBIC fills them before backing off, and latency for everything else on that link goes to hundreds of milliseconds. That's bufferbloat, and the fixes live in the queue (fq_codel, CAKE) as much as in the sender.

Also: TCP is ACK-clocked. The rate of returning ACKs, not a timer, is what releases new data in classic implementations. Modern stacks add explicit pacing on top so a burst of ACKs doesn't produce a burst of packets.
