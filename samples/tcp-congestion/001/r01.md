TCP congestion control is a feedback loop that guesses how much data the network path can hold, and adjusts that guess based on loss, delay, or explicit marks. The sender maintains a congestion window (cwnd) in bytes; it may have at most `min(cwnd, receiver_window)` bytes unacknowledged in flight. Everything else is the policy for moving cwnd up and down.

The classic loss-based algorithm (Reno, and its descendants) runs in four states:

- **Slow start.** cwnd begins around 10 segments and doubles every RTT (+1 MSS per ACK). It is exponential growth, badly named; the point is that it starts small. It exits when cwnd crosses `ssthresh` or a loss occurs.
- **Congestion avoidance.** cwnd grows by roughly 1 MSS per RTT — additive increase. This is the steady-state probe for more bandwidth.
- **Fast retransmit.** Three duplicate ACKs mean a segment is missing but later ones arrived, so the path is not dead. Resend immediately instead of waiting for the retransmission timer.
- **Fast recovery.** On that loss, halve cwnd and ssthresh and continue from there rather than dropping to 1. A retransmission timeout (RTO), which signals a much worse event, does drop cwnd to 1 MSS and re-enters slow start.

The increase/decrease pair is AIMD: additive increase, multiplicative decrease. Chiu and Jain showed AIMD converges to fair sharing among flows with the same RTT, which is why the shape survived. Its two known weaknesses are that throughput scales as `1/(RTT·√p)` for loss rate `p`, so long-RTT flows lose to short-RTT flows on the same bottleneck, and that filling a buffer until it drops is the definition of the loss signal, which means loss-based TCP deliberately keeps queues full (bufferbloat).

Modern algorithms differ in what they treat as the congestion signal:

| Algorithm | Signal | Growth | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reno / NewReno | Packet loss | Linear per RTT | Baseline; too slow to fill high bandwidth-delay paths |
| CUBIC | Packet loss | Cubic function of time since last loss | Linux default since 2006; RTT-independent growth, fills fat pipes |
| Vegas | Rising RTT | Linear | Backs off before loss; starves against loss-based flows sharing a link |
| BBR (v1–v3) | Measured bottleneck bandwidth and min RTT | Model-based, paced | Google/YouTube default; ignores loss as a signal, targets BDP not buffer-full |
| DCTCP | ECN mark fraction | Proportional to marking rate | Datacenter only; needs switch support and shallow thresholds |

Two mechanisms sit alongside cwnd and matter in practice. ECN lets a router mark a packet instead of dropping it, and the receiver echoes the mark back, so the sender reacts to congestion without losing data; L4S extends this to a much finer-grained signal. Pacing spreads a window's worth of packets over an RTT instead of sending them back to back, which BBR requires and CUBIC benefits from, and is why the Linux fq qdisc pairs with these algorithms.

The part people usually miss: congestion control is separate from flow control. The receiver window protects a slow receiver, cwnd protects the network, and the sender obeys whichever is smaller. QUIC reimplements all of this in userspace over UDP with the same algorithms, mostly because middleboxes made changing TCP itself impossible.
