TCP congestion control is a feedback loop that guesses the network's spare capacity: the sender keeps a congestion window (`cwnd`) capping unacknowledged bytes in flight, raises it while ACKs keep arriving, and cuts it when it sees a congestion signal. Throughput is roughly `min(cwnd, receiver_window) / RTT`, so the whole game is picking `cwnd`. Nothing in the network tells the sender the right number; loss, delay, or ECN marks are the only evidence it gets.

The classic Reno state machine has four parts:

- **Slow start.** `cwnd` starts at ~10 MSS and grows by 1 MSS per ACK, doubling each RTT. Exponential probing to find the rough scale of the pipe fast. Exits on loss or on crossing `ssthresh`.
- **Congestion avoidance.** `cwnd` grows by about 1 MSS per RTT (additive increase). Cautious probing once you're near capacity.
- **Fast retransmit / fast recovery.** Three duplicate ACKs mean a segment was lost but packets are still flowing. Retransmit immediately, halve `cwnd` and `ssthresh` (multiplicative decrease), continue in congestion avoidance without draining the pipe.
- **Retransmission timeout.** No ACK at all within the RTO. Assume catastrophe: `ssthresh = cwnd/2`, `cwnd = 1 MSS`, back to slow start. This is the expensive path, which is why SACK and RACK exist to convert timeouts into fast recoveries.

The additive-increase/multiplicative-decrease rule is what makes competing flows converge on a fair share: increase adds the same absolute amount to every flow, decrease removes a proportional amount, so the flow with the larger window gives up more each cut.

Loss-based control has a structural flaw. It only backs off after filling a queue, so on links with deep buffers it drives latency up to hundreds of milliseconds while throughput looks fine, which is bufferbloat. That drove the newer variants:

| Variant | Signal | Behaviour | Where used |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reno / NewReno | Loss | AIMD, halve on loss | Baseline, still in specs |
| CUBIC | Loss | Cubic growth from the last loss point; RTT-independent | Linux default, most of the web |
| BBR | Bandwidth + RTT estimates | Models bottleneck rate and min RTT, paces to it, ignores loss | Google, YouTube, QUIC deployments |
| Vegas | Queuing delay | Backs off before loss; loses to loss-based flows sharing a link | Research, niche |
| DCTCP | ECN mark fraction | Proportional cut, near-empty queues | Datacenters with ECN-capable switches |

Two supporting mechanisms matter as much as the window algorithm: ECN lets routers mark packets instead of dropping them, giving a congestion signal without a retransmit, and pacing spreads a window's worth of packets across the RTT instead of releasing them as a burst, which BBR requires and CUBIC benefits from.
