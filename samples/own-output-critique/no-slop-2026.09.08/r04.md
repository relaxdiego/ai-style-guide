You can't point at the line because the defect is in what's absent, and absence has no line number. Review checks whether the diff does what it claims. Every one of these survives that check.

Concrete differences in what I produce:

- I write against the spec in your message, not against the invariants the rest of the system assumes. The function is correct on its own terms and silently violates a rule that lives three files away and was never written down.
- I put abstraction boundaries where the current code repeats itself, because that's what I can see. Maintainers put them where change is expected. So I merge two things that look alike today and will fork next quarter, and I add a parameter where you needed two separate implementations.
- I add null checks, try/except, and fallbacks for cases I have no belief about. Each one is a claim that the case can occur. Later you have to disprove that claim before you can delete the branch, and you can't, so it stays forever.
- The polish is uniform. In human code, the careful parts are marked: odd names, an extra comment, a defensive structure. Mine reads equally considered everywhere, so you get no signal about which part is actually hard.
- My comments restate the code instead of recording why the code is that way, because I usually don't know why. That reason is then unrecoverable.
- I reach for generic names: handler, process, manager, data, result. Each is acceptable in isolation. Collectively they erase your domain vocabulary, and the codebase stops teaching new readers what it's about.
- When the domain is irregular, I smooth it into something that looks regular. The irregularity comes back later as a special case bolted onto a shape that can't hold it.
- I generate a new helper more readily than I search for the existing one, so near-duplicate utilities accumulate.

What actually helps: tell me the invariants and which parts you expect to change, before I write. Ban error handling for failure modes I can't name out loud. Ask me what I assumed and require the answers to land in comments. And when I hand something back, ask which part I was least sure about, since that's the location you were trying to find.
