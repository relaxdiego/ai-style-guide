The defect is distributed, which is why there's no line to point at: each line is defensible, and what's missing is a decision. Absence has no line number.

Concretely, what I produce differs from code written by someone who will maintain it:

- **I normalize defensively instead of picking one representation.** The function accepts a string or a list or None and copes with all three. Reviewer sees robustness. Six months later nobody knows which shape actually arrives, so no branch can be deleted and every new caller adds a fourth shape.
- **Null checks and try/except are spread evenly rather than placed at a boundary.** Uniform error handling carries zero information about which failures matter. A human puts validation at the edge and then trusts the interior; that trust is the thing you maintain against.
- **My abstractions are parameterized on the dimension that never varies.** I've seen what the average codebase generalizes; I haven't seen your change history. So the config object is pluggable and the retry policy is hardcoded, when your last two years of commits were all retry policy.
- **Names are mechanically accurate and domain-empty.** `process_batch`, `handle_response`, `result`. Nothing is wrong. You also can't grep for the concept, and the reader gets no clue about what the code is for.
- **Comments state what the line does, never what I rejected.** The reader can't tell what's safe to change, so they change nothing and add beside it.
- **Tests pin my implementation rather than your behavior.** They pass now, they break on refactor, and they don't fail when the behavior you care about regresses.
- **The code is internally consistent and slightly off from its neighbors.** Unless I read the surrounding files, I match my own conventions. Every divergence is too small to flag in review; the sum is that nothing composes.
- **There are no dead ends.** Human code carries evidence of an approach tried and abandoned, and that evidence reads as constraint. Mine reads like the first draft was the final one, so it looks like nothing was ruled out because nothing was.

What fixes it is telling me things review doesn't check. Name the invariant and where it's enforced, say which shape the data actually has instead of letting me accept all of them, point me at the file whose conventions I should match, and say what's expected to change so I generalize on that dimension and hardcode the rest. Asking me to delete a branch is a better prompt than asking me to add a case.
