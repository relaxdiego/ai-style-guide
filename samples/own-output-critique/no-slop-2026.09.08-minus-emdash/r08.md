You can't point at the line because nothing is wrong at the line. Every one of these is a property of the relationship between lines, and review reads diffs.

The specific mechanisms:

- **No record of what was rejected.** A person's code accumulates around incidents: a retry with a weird backoff, a null check that only fires on one vendor's response. My comments say what the code does. They don't say which obvious alternative was tried and failed. So when you go to change something, you can't tell what's deliberate and what's arbitrary. Everything looks equally intentional, which means nothing is safe to delete.
- **Defensiveness spread evenly instead of concentrated at a boundary.** I validate at the same rate in every function. Each one reads as careful. The result is that no single place establishes the invariant, so you can never prove a given check is redundant, and the checks stay forever.
- **Abstraction factored on resemblance rather than on what changes together.** Three call sites that look alike get a helper. Then one needs to differ and the helper grows a boolean parameter, then a second one. The original extraction passed review because at that moment the three really were identical.
- **Vocabulary from the framework, not from your domain.** `process`, `handle`, `Manager`, `data`, `result`. Each name is defensible alone. Collectively there is no set of words you can think in, so every reading starts from the types.
- **Ambiguity resolved silently.** Where you would have left a TODO or asked someone, I pick the plausible reading and render it as settled. The uncertainty is real and it is invisible in the artifact.
- **Uniform polish.** In a codebase people wrote, the scruffy parts sit near the boring problems and the careful parts near the hard ones, and that difference tells you where to slow down. Mine is level everywhere, so you have to read all of it at the same rate.
- **Functions cut to a length rather than at a boundary.** The split lands where the line count said, not where the concept ends, so the two halves share four parameters and neither is comprehensible alone.

What changes it is asking me for the decisions, not the code: which alternative I rejected and why, where the single validation boundary is, what your domain calls the nouns. Reviewing my output for "is this correct" will keep passing, because it is correct. Review it for "could I delete this line and know what breaks."
