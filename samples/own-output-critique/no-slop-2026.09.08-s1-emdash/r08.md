Review reads one piece at a time, and the problems in my code live in the relationships between pieces. Each function defends itself under scrutiny; the cost lands on whoever has to change three of them together.

The specific mechanisms:

- **Defensive code with no incident behind it.** Null checks, try/except wrappers, fallback defaults that never fired for a real bug. A human reading it assumes something once returned null there. You can't delete it, because you can't prove the reason isn't real. Every one is a permanent tax on reasoning about that path.
- **Uniform care.** Human code has asymmetries: the hard part got three revisions, the boring part got one. That tells a maintainer where to look. Mine is evenly polished throughout, so you have to treat all of it as though it matters.
- **Abstraction placed where the prompt pointed, not where change arrives.** I have no guess about which direction the next requirement comes from, so the flexibility is spread evenly or put where you happened to mention it. You go to change one behavior and edit seven files, none of which is wrong.
- **False symmetry.** I make three handlers look parallel because parallel reads well, even when one has a different failure mode. The difference that matters gets flattened into matching structure.
- **Mechanical names.** `processData`, `handleResponse`, `validateResult`. They survive review because they're accurate. They fail in month six because the words in your head are domain words, and grep finds nothing.
- **Tests that assert the implementation.** They pass, they cover lines, and they encode incidental behavior as though it were a requirement. A legitimate refactor breaks forty of them, and none of the forty tells you what was actually promised.
- **Pattern completion.** Three similar things exist, so I build the fourth to match, including when the fourth should have been a special case with a comment saying why.
- **The median solution.** I reach for the most common approach in the training distribution, which is usually the tutorial version, sized for a system smaller than yours.
- **Volume.** I write more code than the problem needs. Each line reviews fine. Nobody reviews mass.

What actually helps is asking for the reasoning where it's absent: make me state which change I expect next and where the flexibility lives, delete every guard I can't tie to a real failure, and write tests against the requirement rather than against what I just wrote. Also tell me your constraints that differ from the median system, because I default to the median one otherwise.
