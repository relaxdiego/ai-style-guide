The code answers the prompt instead of joining the codebase, and the damage is an absence rather than a defect: nothing in the file tells you which decisions were forced and which were arbitrary. When you edit it later you can't tell what's safe to change, so every change costs a full re-derivation of reasoning that was never written down. Reviewers don't catch this because review checks whether the code is right, and it is.

The specific mechanisms:

- **Everything is written at the same level of care.** Real code has scarred places where someone lost two days to a bug, and boring places nobody thought about. I produce uniform density: the same defensiveness around a pure function as around a network write. The reader loses the signal that says "this line is here for a reason, be careful."

- **I re-derive abstractions that already exist elsewhere in your repo.** The new one is internally consistent, slightly different from yours, and now there are two ways to do the thing with no rule for choosing. Each is fine alone.

- **Comments say what the code does, not what was rejected.** No record of the alternative that looked better and failed, the vendor bug being worked around, the ordering that matters. Those are exactly the facts a maintainer needs and cannot recover from reading.

- **I complete symmetries nobody asked for.** A `get` gets a `set`. Three cases get a fourth. An interface gets built for one implementation. Every unrequested branch is now permanent surface that must be understood, tested and kept true.

- **Names come from my sentence, not your domain.** `Manager`, `handle`, `process`, `result`, `config`, or a precise new term invented on the spot that nobody on your team says out loud. Your vocabulary drifts one word per file.

- **Dangerous operations look like ordinary ones.** Nothing in the formatting or structure distinguishes an idempotent local call from one that charges a customer. I have no fear, so the code encodes no fear.

- **Tests mirror the implementation rather than the requirement.** They pass, they break on every refactor, and they catch nothing. This is the largest hidden maintenance cost and the hardest to see in review.

- **Nothing looks unfinished.** A human leaves a `TODO`, a rough patch, a function that obviously wants a second pass. I emit a smooth surface everywhere, including the parts I understood least, so you can't find where to look first.

What actually fixes it is making me write down the things above rather than asking for better code. Tell me to cite the existing pattern I'm following and name the file, to leave a comment on any line whose form was constrained by something outside the file, to flag what I was unsure about instead of smoothing it, and to write tests against the requirement without reading my implementation. Rejecting unrequested generality on sight helps more than any review comment about style.
