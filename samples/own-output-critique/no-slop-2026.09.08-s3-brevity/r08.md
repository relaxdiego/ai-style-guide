None of the lines is wrong. The defect is missing information: my code carries no record of what I rejected, so every line reads as equally deliberate and you can't tell which ones are safe to change. Maintenance is mostly deciding what you're allowed to break, and I hand you a file with no answer to that.

The specific things that produce the feeling:

- **Uniform confidence.** A human writes gnarlier code where the problem is gnarly, and that unevenness tells you where the danger is. I write the tricky part and the boilerplate in the same register, so nothing warns you.
- **Abstraction placed by convention, not after seeing duplication.** You extract a helper on the third copy, and the helper fits because three real cases shaped it. I extract it immediately because helper-shaped code is what I've seen most of. It fits the fourth case badly and nobody knows why it exists.
- **Guards for states that can't occur.** I pattern-match "robust" rather than checking reachability. A year later you can't tell whether a nil check covers a real path, so you keep it forever, and it accumulates.
- **Comments that restate the code.** `// validate the response` instead of "the vendor returns 200 on failure". The reason is the only part you'll need later, and it's the part I drop.
- **Tests that assert what the code does, not what was required.** They pass permanently and catch nothing. Refactoring breaks all of them at once, which teaches you not to refactor.
- **One concept, different vocabulary per file.** I don't hold the whole system in view, so I re-derive the same idea locally and name it three ways. Grep stops working.
- **Ambiguity resolved silently.** Where a human would have asked you which reading was intended, I picked one and made it look settled.

Review doesn't catch any of this because review compares the diff against correctness and style, not against the change you'll make next quarter. If you want it fixed at the source, ask me to state the alternative I rejected and why, mark the guesses I made about your intent, and hold off on abstractions until there are actual repeated call sites. That output is uglier and more useful.
