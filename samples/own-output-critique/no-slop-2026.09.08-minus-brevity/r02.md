Each line is defensible; the cost only appears at the moment of change, which is not what review measures. Review asks "is this correct and readable." Maintenance asks "when I change X, does this change with it." I optimize hard for the first because that's the signal I get.

Concretely, here's what I do differently from someone who has lived in your codebase:

- **I re-derive instead of reusing.** I write a small helper inline rather than finding the equivalent one three directories over, because a self-contained diff reviews well. Then your shared helper changes and my copy silently doesn't.
- **I spread error handling evenly.** I don't know which inputs are already validated, so I check everything at equal weight. A person who knows the system puts one check at the boundary where untrusted data enters. My version hides where the real boundary is, and nothing is emphasized because everything is.
- **I write tests against the code I just wrote.** They assert on the structure I picked rather than the behavior you need. They pass forever, miss the regression, and break during refactors that changed no behavior.
- **My names describe local mechanics, not your domain.** `process_items`, `handle_response`, `result`. Accurate, so review passes. Names are how you search and how you reason about the system, and generic ones make that index useless.
- **I comment the what.** Review reads that as thoroughness. The comment you need in six months is "retries 3x because upstream returns 503 during their nightly deploy."
- **I leave no record of rejected options.** I pick among three viable designs and ship only the winner. When you hit the constraint that killed option B, you can't tell whether I ever saw it.
- **I'm consistent with myself, not with you.** Each file I write is internally coherent. Across sessions I don't remember what idiom I chose last time, so two files diverge without either being wrong.

What fixes it is changing what you ask for. Tell me the constraint, not just the requirement ("this runs inside the request handler, everything upstream is already validated"), point me at the existing module you want me to reuse, and ask me to state what I rejected and why before I write. For tests, describe the behavior you want verified and let me write those tests before the implementation, so they can't mirror it.
