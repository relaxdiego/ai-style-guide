The defects sit in what's absent and in relations between files, while review reads present lines inside one diff. That's why nothing is pointable.

- **No record of why.** I emit the choice without the alternatives I discarded. Later you can't tell which constant is arbitrary, which ordering is required, which branch guards a case that actually happens. Every edit means re-deriving the original decision from scratch.
- **Abstractions extracted from one instance.** A person factors out after the third repetition, so the parameters are the things that actually varied. I pattern-match on the first one and guess: generalized along a dimension that never changes, hardcoded on the one that will.
- **Local consistency, global drift.** I infer conventions from the 200 lines around the edit. Ten sessions later you have three retry helpers and three error-wrapping styles, each defensible where it sits, and no rule that tells you which to use next.
- **Defensive code with no threat model.** Null checks and fallback defaults on paths where the invariant already holds. Reviewers almost never reject an extra check, but it turns a loud crash into a quiet wrong value and destroys the information about which invariants are real.
- **Flat emphasis.** I comment and structure the dangerous 5% exactly like the boring 95%. Human code, even careless human code, gets denser and more hedged where the author was nervous. That signal is how you know where to slow down, and mine doesn't have it.
- **Completing the shape.** A setter because there's a getter, a fourth case because there were three, a config option nobody requested. It's all correct, and you maintain it forever.

What changes it: tell me the invariants and who enforces them, tell me when to crash instead of defaulting, and make me write down the rejected alternative in the commit message or a comment. Also ask for the ugly version first and only let me factor out after you've seen the repetition.
