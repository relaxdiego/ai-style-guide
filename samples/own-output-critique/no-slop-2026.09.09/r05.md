The difference is that my code carries no record of what I rejected, and review only checks what's there.

When you write a function, the odd guard clause exists because something broke in production, the retry count is 3 because 5 was too slow, the abstraction stops at that boundary because you tried extending it once and it got ugly. None of that is in the diff, but it's in your head, and it comes back when you edit. My code has none of it. Every line was produced with the same uniform confidence, whether it was forced by a real constraint or picked from three equally plausible options. So a year later you can't tell which lines are arbitrary and which are holding something up, and the safest move becomes changing nothing. That's the feeling you can't point at: the problem isn't in any line, it's the absence of a signal that would let you rank the lines.

Specific mechanisms that produce it:

- **Abstractions placed by resemblance, not by observed change.** You factor out code after editing the same thing in three places. I factor out code that looks alike. My boundaries land on syntactic similarity, so when a real requirement arrives it cuts across the abstraction instead of along it. Fixing that means moving a boundary, which is the most expensive edit there is.
- **Silent resolution of ambiguity.** Where your spec was underspecified, I picked a reading and wrote confident code. No TODO, no question, no comment. Null handling, empty-list semantics, timezone assumptions, what happens on partial failure: all decided, none marked.
- **Local coherence, global drift.** I match the style of the file in front of me. I don't know that this is the fourth error-handling convention in the repo, because each one looked fine next to its neighbors.
- **Vocabulary that's plausible instead of yours.** I'll write `fetchAccountRecord` when your team says `pull subscriber`. It reads fine and it breaks grep, and it makes every future reader translate.
- **Completeness as a reflex.** I handle branches nobody asked about and write defensive code for conditions that can't occur. Reviewers read that as thorough. Later it's code you can't delete because you can't prove nothing depends on it.
- **Comments that restate mechanics.** `// increment the counter`. The why is the only part that was ever expensive to recover, and it's the part I don't have.

What actually helps: make me list the alternatives I considered and why I discarded them, in the PR description or in comments at the decision points. Reject abstractions I introduce that you didn't ask for. Tell me to mark every guess explicitly rather than pick silently, and give me your domain terms up front so I stop inventing near-misses.
