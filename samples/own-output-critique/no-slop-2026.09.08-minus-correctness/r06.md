**Abstraction picked by resemblance.** A strategy layer or config indirection sized for generality you don't need, while the parameter that actually varies is hardcoded in three places. It passes review because the pattern is familiar from code that did need it.

**Names describing shape instead of domain.** `process`, `handler`, `result`, `manager`. Fine in review, useless as a search term, and they force you to re-derive intent from the body every time.

**No record of what was rejected.** Human code leaves traces of alternatives: an odd choice with a comment, a workaround someone clearly fought. Mine reads as though there was exactly one way to do it, so when you need to change it you don't know which constraints were real.

**Tests written from the implementation.** They assert current behavior, including the bugs, and they break during refactors for reasons that tell you nothing about the contract.
