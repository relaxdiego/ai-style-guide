The defect is spread across lines that are each defensible, which is why review passes and why you can't point at one.

**No record of what the code is defending against.** I write every function as if the codebase started this morning. Your retry loop exists because a vendor 502s under load; mine exists because retry loops are good practice. They look identical. Six months later you can't tell which guards, null checks, and fallbacks are protecting against something real, so you can't delete any of them, and the file only accretes.

**Effort spread evenly over unequal risk.** A human puts careful error handling on the payment path and three plain lines on the config loader. That variation tells the next reader where the danger is. I tend to apply the same validation, logging, and docstring density everywhere, which erases the signal, and you have to rebuild the risk model yourself every time you open the file.

**Abstractions fitted to two examples.** I generalize on the second occurrence, and the parameter I extract matches the two cases in front of me rather than the direction your domain actually varies. It reads clean at review. The next requirement lands across the parameter, and someone adds a boolean flag.

**Names describing the code rather than your business.** `process_items`, `handle_response`, `DataManager`. Not wrong, so not flagged, but they carry no domain meaning, so the file teaches you nothing and must be read line by line.

**Written for the diff, not the file.** I optimize for a self-contained, easy-to-review change, which favors a new near-duplicate helper over editing the existing one.

What actually changes the output: tell me the constraint behind the request, not just the request, and I'll write the reason into the code. Ask me to mark which branches are guesses about behavior I couldn't verify. Tell me to inline until the third real use, and to name things in your vocabulary.
