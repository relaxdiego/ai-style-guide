Most likely: the code is locally correct and globally foreign, and review only checks locally. Specific mechanisms, roughly in order of how often they're the culprit:

- **I complete patterns that the problem doesn't have.** If there's a `get_x` I tend to write `set_x`. If three enum cases are handled I handle the fourth even when it's unreachable. Review sees thoroughness. You later have to prove the extra branches are dead before you can touch anything.
- **I generalize on the first call site.** A human waits for the third. So you get a helper with two config parameters and one caller, and the parameter names encode a generality nobody asked for. Deleting it requires reading it, and reading it costs more than the code it saved.
- **Uniform polish erases risk signal.** Human code tells you where the author was nervous: a defensive check here, a terse line there, a comment at the one weird spot. Mine is evenly finished everywhere, so nothing stands out and you can't find the line that's wrong, because the wrongness is distributional.
- **Error handling that converts crashes into wrong answers.** `try/except` with a logged warning and a fallback default passes review as "handles errors." It costs you the stack trace at 2am.
- **Comments restate the line instead of justifying it.** What the code does is visible. Why this instead of the obvious alternative is the thing you need in six months, and I usually don't know it, because you didn't tell me.
- **Tests written from the implementation, not the contract.** I just wrote the code, so I assert its shape. Those tests pass forever, catch nothing, and break on every refactor.
- **New vocabulary for existing concepts.** Your codebase says `account`; I write `user_record`, handle the retry one layer lower than the surrounding modules do, and re-solve something already solved in a sibling file I never read.

The fix on your side is cheap: point me at two or three neighbor files and say "match these," and say "add nothing that isn't required by the spec, no speculative parameters, no new abstractions with one caller." On errors, tell me to let it crash unless you name a recovery. Those three constraints remove most of the list.
