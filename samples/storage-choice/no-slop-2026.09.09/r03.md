SQLite for the on-device store. Postgres isn't an embedded database: it's a server process with a data directory, a port, and a startup cost you'd have to ship and supervise on every user's machine. For a genuinely local-first app (data lives on the device, works offline, syncs later), that's the whole decision. Postgres belongs on the sync server side, if you have one.

The fork worth checking before you commit: "local-first" sometimes means "self-hosted single-node web app," where the database runs on a box the user controls and clients hit it over the network. If that's your case, Postgres is the right call and SQLite will hurt you the moment you have concurrent writers.

What actually locks you in isn't the engine:

- **Sync and conflict resolution.** Last-write-wins vs. CRDTs vs. an operation log. Changing this later means rewriting the schema and migrating every client's data. Decide it now.
- **Schema migration on clients you don't control.** Old versions keep running offline for months. You need forward-compatible reads and versioned migrations from day one.
- **Where identity and authorization live.** Cheap to get right early, expensive to retrofit.

Swapping SQLite for something else later is a week of work. Swapping your merge semantics is a rewrite. Spend your worry there.

If you want concrete options for the sync layer: Turso/libSQL and Electric give you SQLite with server replication, Automerge and Yjs give you CRDTs with a storage backend you pick.
