SQLite, unless you have a specific reason you can name. Local-first means the database lives on the user's machine, and Postgres there means shipping an installer, supervising a server process, owning a data directory, and doing `pg_upgrade` across versions. That's a permanent support burden for a machine with one user on it. SQLite is a library in your binary: WAL mode, `STRICT` tables, done.

What would flip me to Postgres: you need many OS processes writing concurrently at high rates (SQLite gives one writer, many readers), or you depend on something with no real SQLite equivalent (PostGIS, pgvector at scale, arrays/ranges, `LISTEN/NOTIFY`). If you also have a server side, the answer is both: Postgres on the server, SQLite on each client. Those are different jobs and picking the same engine for both buys you nothing.

The decision that's actually expensive to reverse isn't the engine. It's how you sync and resolve conflicts: CRDTs, an append-only oplog you replay, or last-write-wins per field. That choice constrains your schema everywhere and is miserable to retrofit. SQLite to Postgres later is a routine migration; a wrong merge model is a rewrite. If you want, tell me what the sync story is and I'll poke at that instead.
