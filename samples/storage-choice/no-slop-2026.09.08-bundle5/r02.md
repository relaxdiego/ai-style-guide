SQLite on the device, and Postgres only shows up if you also run a server. For a local-first app that is the standard pairing, and it isn't a compromise: SQLite is an embedded library with no process to supervise, no port to bind, a single file you can copy, and it is the only one of the two that actually works with the network off. Postgres in the same slot means shipping and supervising a server process per machine, or running PGlite in WASM, which buys you Postgres syntax at the cost of a much younger runtime and a much larger binary.

Three things would flip that, and none is likely for local-first: many concurrent writers against one database (SQLite in WAL mode gives you many readers but one writer at a time), a dependency on Postgres-only machinery like `pgvector`, PostGIS, or logical replication, or a dataset per user that outgrows what you want to hold on a laptop. What you should actually worry about is sync, not storage. Choosing between last-writer-wins, an operation log, and CRDTs determines your schema, your primary keys, your tombstones, and whether you can ever merge two divergent copies, and unlike the engine choice it is close to impossible to change later. Pick a sync design first, keep the SQL plain (no engine-specific types, no stored procedures), and swapping engines stays a few days of work rather than a rewrite.
