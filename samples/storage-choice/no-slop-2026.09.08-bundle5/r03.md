SQLite, unless you already know you need a server. For a local-first app the database runs in your process, on the user's disk, with no daemon to install, supervise, or version-match. Postgres in that slot means shipping and babysitting a server per machine, which is the kind of thing that turns into support tickets about port conflicts and stale data directories. Performance is not the deciding factor either way at local-first scale; operations is.

Three things would flip me to Postgres:

- **A shared backend that runs the same schema and queries.** If the sync server is Postgres, using SQLite on the client means two dialects and two migration paths. Sometimes that is fine, sometimes it is the whole cost of the project.
- **Concurrent writers from separate processes.** SQLite in WAL mode handles one writer at a time and many readers. Several OS processes writing hard against one file will serialize and eventually time out.
- **Postgres-only features you actually depend on**: real types beyond SQLite's five, `jsonb` with indexing, full-text search you do not want to hand-roll, `pgvector`, stored procedures.

What you should worry about more than the engine choice is the sync and conflict model, because that is the part you cannot swap later. Migrating a schema from SQLite to Postgres is a week of type-mapping and rewritten queries; changing how conflicting offline edits merge means rewriting the data model and reconciling every record already on users' disks. If you keep your queries behind a thin data layer and avoid SQLite's looser typing, the storage decision stays reversible.
