SQLite for the local store. For a local-first app the local store must be an embedded library in your process with no daemon, no port, no user account, and no upgrade dance on the user's machine, and that rules out Postgres before any feature comparison starts. The Postgres-shaped options for local-first (PGlite compiled to WASM, or shipping a per-user postmaster) exist and work, but you take on a much larger binary, a much smaller pool of people who have debugged it in production, and worse mobile stories, and you get that in exchange for features (rich types, real concurrency across processes, extensions) that a single-user local database rarely uses.

The decision you actually can get wrong is sync, not storage. Pick how writes from two devices reconcile before you pick the engine: last-write-wins per field, CRDTs, or an operation log replayed against a server. That choice constrains your schema hard, since CRDT-backed systems want you storing operations or version vectors rather than plain rows, and retrofitting it later is a rewrite of every table plus a migration of every user's existing data. Postgres enters legitimately as the *server* side of that sync, paired with SQLite on each device, which is the common arrangement and is not the either/or you posed. The narrow cases where I'd reconsider SQLite locally: you need multiple OS processes writing the same database concurrently (SQLite gives you one writer at a time, and WAL mode softens but does not remove this), you depend on a Postgres-only extension such as PostGIS or pgvector at scale on-device, or "local" means a shared box on a LAN with several real users, which is not local-first.
